== BUILT TO STAY QUIET UNTIL IT MATTERS ==

* One alert per approach, not one per tick. Price chopping around a wall
  is a single alert. The wall re-arms only after price backs clearly
  away, and that distance scales with how fast the market is moving.

* Breaks that are real. A break needs several straight 1-minute closes
  beyond the wall, so a wick or a failed breakout does not count. A
  broken wall stays quiet for the rest of the day.

* Only the walls that matter to you. A strength slider ranks each wall
  against the same symbol's walls over the last 30 days at the same time
  of day, so "Strong" means the same thing on SPY as on NDX. Set it to
  Strong and the small walls never ping you.

* Three noise filters. Responsive gives the earliest warning, Balanced
  is the recommended default, and Conservative sends the fewest alerts.

* Context in every alert. The price, how far it is from the wall, the
  wall's strength, which test of the day this is, and, where our sample
  is large enough, how often walls like it have broken within the hour.


== WHAT A DAY LOOKS LIKE ==

SPX spent much of Tuesday, October 13 around 7,800 and crossed it more
than once. A replay of that session with the default settings (both
walls, hits and breaks, Balanced) sends four alerts all day:

  10:02 AM   Put wall 7,800 hit       SPX 7,802.82
  11:30 AM   Put wall 7,800 broke     SPX 7,788.26
  12:01 PM   Call wall 7,800 hit      SPX 7,796.64
   3:11 PM   Call wall 7,800 broke    SPX 7,809.30

The level went from support to resistance, and you would have heard
about each turn once.
