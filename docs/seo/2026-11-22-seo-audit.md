## Shipped

### 1. The footer no longer links every page into a redirect

The footer's "Platform" link pointed at `/dashboard` on 125 of the 127 pages crawled. For anyone without a plan, Googlebot included, `/dashboard` answers with a 307 to `/spx-gamma-levels` and an `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` header. That made it the only site-wide internal link into a redirect: every page passed part of its link value to a URL marked noindex. The October audit fixed the same pattern in the homepage and `/about` module grids but not here.

The link now renders for Basic and Pro members only. Everyone else already has the free version of the same surface one line below it ("Gamma Terminal", `/chart`), which is where `core/publicToolHref.ts` sends signed-out visitors from `/dashboard` anyway. Members see exactly what they saw before.

The re-crawl then showed one page still linking `/dashboard`: `/about`, whose hero button ("Open Dashboard") and closing button ("Launch Dashboard") were hard-coded, while the module grid on the same page already used the public-link rule. Both buttons now go through that rule: members still open the dashboard, everyone else goes straight to `/chart` instead of through the redirect. After both fixes, no crawled page links `/dashboard` (it was 125).

`tests/gatedLinks.test.ts` fails the build if any footer link to a gated route renders for signed-out visitors, or if the homepage or `/about` hard-codes a `/dashboard` link again.

### 2. A gamma squeeze explainer: `/education/gamma-squeeze-explained`

"Gamma squeeze" and its variants ("what is a gamma squeeze", "gamma squeeze explained", "gamma squeeze vs short squeeze") are the most-searched terms in this topic space, and no page on the site used the phrase. The mechanism was spread across the negative-gamma and call-wall explainers under other names, so the site had the authority to answer the search but no page answering it. This is the one-page-per-intent rule working in the other direction: an uncovered intent, not a second page for a covered one. The negative-gamma explainer keeps the regime, the call-wall explainer keeps the level, and the new page owns the event.

What it covers: the definition, the hedging loop step by step, why short-dated options concentrate it, gamma squeeze vs short squeeze (a table), single stocks vs indexes, what GEX data can and cannot show, what ends a squeeze, and common misreadings, including the difference from the Squeeze Setup signal. About 2,000 words, so it gets the "On this page" list.

How it stays consistent with the rest of the site:

- The wording is hedged the same way the other explainers are ("modeled", "tends to"), and it passes the wall-break and methodology claim guards.
- Every figure comes from the wall-break study: 737 tests over ten weeks, S&P walls breaking within the hour about 31% of the time and Nasdaq walls about 47-50%, the regime not changing the break rate.
- It says plainly what an open-interest GEX model cannot see in a squeeze: who bought the calls. A call-buying frenzy is exactly when the standard convention (customers sell calls) is wrong, the same limit the wall-break study names as the likeliest reason a wall breaks "unexpectedly".

Wiring: the registry (title 56 characters, description 159), a five-question FAQ mirrored as FAQPage markup, the related-reading graph (six links out, three in), in-text links from the pillar, the negative-gamma, call-wall and Squeeze Setup articles, and the education hub's "Popular explainers" list. The sitemap, `/articles`, the RSS feed, `llms.txt` and on-site search all read the registry, so it appears in each without further edits. It is English only. Readers with another language selected get the English text, as the loader already does for any untranslated article.

### 3. The comparison pages now all link the SpotGamma comparison

The five comparison pages cross-link through their related-reading lists, but `/education/zerogex-vs-spotgamma` shipped last (October 11) and only MenthorQ's list was updated. The comparison against the most-searched competitor name had the fewest internal links of the five: 4 linking pages against 6-9 for the others. The Bullflow, Quant Data and TradeGEX lists now include it. Each list stays at six items, because the related grid is three wide and a seventh card would sit alone on its own row. To make room, each list dropped one explainer that keeps plenty of other inbound links.

### 4. `llms.txt` names the chart indicators, the forecast and the track record

"Is there a gamma levels indicator for TradingView?" is a question people put to assistants nearly word for word, and `llms.txt` named none of the five chart integrations. It now has a "Chart-platform indicators" section built from `core/integrations.ts`, the registry the `/integrations` hub and every landing render from. A sixth platform appears there without an edit, and the free-or-Pro label cannot disagree with the site. The daily forecast and the forecast track record were added to "Free tools".

### 6. Two titles that named nothing anyone searches

- `/track-record`: "Track Record - ZeroGEX" is now "ZeroGEX Track Record: Every Forecast Graded, Misses Included". Someone weighing a subscription searches the brand plus "review", "accuracy" or "track record", and this is the page that answers them.
- `/replay`: "GEX Replay - ZeroGEX" is now "Free Historical GEX Replay: Past Sessions, Minute by Minute", for the "historical gex" and "gex history" searches this free tool answers.

### 7. The empty Quick Starts page is out of the index until it has a video

`/help/quickstarts` has no published video yet. It shows four links to the written guides under a title that promises "Short Video Walkthroughs", and nothing on the site links to it: the Help menu entry and Help Center card wait for the first video, by design. The sitemap was its only way in, so a searcher could land on a video page with no videos. It is now `noindex` and out of the sitemap while no walkthrough is live, and both lift on their own when one is published. `tests/quickStartsIndexing.test.ts` checks that the page and the sitemap agree on that, today and after the first video ships.

## Not done, and why

- **Linking the levels pages' indicator sections to the indicator landings.** The levels pages embed the full TradingView, thinkorswim and NinjaTrader sections but do not link `/tradingview-indicator` and the others, so those landings get few internal links. The landings are the same section with little added, so a link alone would not make them rank, and it would add an exit inside the conversion block of the eight busiest pages. Giving each landing something of its own (setup FAQ, alert recipes) is the content task that would make it worth linking.
- **Retitling `/forecast` for "SPX forecast today".** The page is a dated archive. Today's numbers are on the permalink it links to, so a "forecast today" title would promise what the page does not show.
- **Short help-page descriptions.** Nineteen Platform Guide pages have 48-68 character descriptions. They are product docs with little search demand, and Google writes its own snippet for them. It can wait.
- **Amazonbot.** It is still on the robots.txt keep-out list, deliberately (November 2), and Amazon has no meaningful search share with options traders.
- **More single-stock pages.** The demand is real ("tsla gex", "aapl gamma levels"), but each needs the backend to carry that chain first. NVDA and MSFT are the only stocks it serves today.
- **New comparison pages.** "Volland alternative" and "Unusual Whales alternative" have the same buyer intent as the five existing pages. Every fact on a comparison page has to come from the vendor's own pages and be reviewed before it ships, so they are not drafted here.
- **A footer link for the gamma squeeze page.** Better decided after it has indexed and shown which searches it takes.
